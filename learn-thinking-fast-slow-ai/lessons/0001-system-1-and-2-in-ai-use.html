Two modes of thinking when you use AI

Lesson 1 · Thinking Fast and Slow × AI · about 10 minutes

Mission: notice when fast thinking is accepting or dismissing AI output without checking it.

The core idea

System 1 is fast, automatic, and intuitive. System 2 is slow, effortful, and deliberate. System 2 is supposed to check System 1, but it is lazy and often accepts whatever System 1 proposes (overview).

AI output arrives fluent, confident, and instant. That invites System 1 to accept it without a second look. Research on automation bias finds people over-rely on automated recommendations, and the tendency rises with workload (Goddard et al. systematic review).

Two ways to go wrong

An error of commission is following a wrong AI recommendation. An error of omission is missing a problem the AI did not flag. Both come from letting the system's output stand in for your own check.

Practice

For each scenario, choose the mode most likely driving the response.

1. The AI summary of a 40-page contract says "no termination penalty." It reads cleanly, so you forward it to legal without opening the contract.
  • System 1 drives this
  • System 2 drives this
Correct. Quick, fluent acceptance is System 1 at work.
Not quite. Notice how fast the response happened and whether any checking occurred.
2. The AI gives a confident fact that contradicts something you know, and you stop to check the source before deciding which to believe.
  • System 1 drives this
  • System 2 drives this
Correct. Stopping to check a conflict is System 2 at work.
Not quite. Stopping to check a source takes effort, which is the System 2 signature.
3. You reject an AI draft in one second because the first sentence sounds generic, without reading the rest.
  • System 1 drives this
  • System 2 drives this
Correct. A snap rejection on a single sentence is System 1.
Not quite. A snap judgment from one sentence is System 1.

Takeaway

The fluency of AI output is a System 1 signal, not evidence of accuracy. Before acting on AI output, name the one claim that matters most and verify it against a source you control.

Try it this week: pick one AI answer you would normally accept, and check its single most important claim.
