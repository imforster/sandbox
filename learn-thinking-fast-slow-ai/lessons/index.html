Thinking Fast and Slow × AI

Course index · 10 lessons · Kahneman's dual-process ideas applied to how people use and judge AI output

Mission

Improve my own AI use by noticing when fast thinking accepts or dismisses AI output without checking it, and by building a short check to run before acting on it. Starting point: new to the material. See MISSION.md.

Lessons

  1. 01System 1 and System 2 in AI useThe two modes of thought, automation bias, and why fluent output feels right.
  2. 02Anchoring: the first number winsHow a number in your prompt steers the answer, and how an answer steers you.
  3. 03Availability: what comes to mindWhy one vivid AI failure can distort your sense of how reliable it is.
  4. 04Representativeness and base ratesWhen a judgment fits a stereotype and ignores how common the outcome is.
  5. 05Framing: same facts, different answersHow wording changes AI verdicts, and the opposite-framing test.
  6. 06Fluency feels trueWhy polished text, repetition, and citations lower your guard.
  7. 07Confirmation bias meets sycophancyLeading questions get agreement, and how to ask for the strongest counterargument.
  8. 08Overconfidence and calibrationWhy longer explanations raise confidence without raising accuracy.
  9. 09Substitution and what you seeAnswers to easier questions, and answers built on missing information.
  10. 10A pre-action check for AI outputCapstone: one short check that combines the lessons before a decision.
