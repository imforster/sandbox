Anchoring Bias
The pull an initial number or suggestion exerts on later estimates, even when it is irrelevant. In AI use it runs both ways: a number in your prompt steers the answer, and the answer steers you.

Automation bias Bias
Over-reliance on automated recommendations. Produces errors of commission (following a wrong recommendation) and errors of omission (missing what the system did not flag).
Lesson 0001
Availability heuristic Bias
Judging how common or likely something is by how easily examples come to mind. Recent or vivid cases feel more frequent than they are.
Lesson 0003
B
Base rate neglect Bias
Underweighting how common an outcome or category is when a description seems to fit a type. The engineer-lawyer study is the classic demonstration.
Lesson 0004
C
Calibration
The match between stated confidence and actual accuracy. A well-calibrated judge is right about 80 percent of the time when saying 80 percent.
Lesson 0008
Cognitive ease
The feeling that information is easy to process. Kahneman's term for why familiar, fluent statements feel true and safe.
Lesson 0006
Confirmation bias Bias
Seeking and weighing evidence that supports what you already believe. Leading questions to an AI are an easy way to get it without effort.

Error of omission

Fluency
How easily information is processed. In this course, a signal of familiarity, not of accuracy.

Framing effect Bias

Illusory truth effect Bias

Opposite-framing test
Asking the same question in reverse (for example, risky? then safe?) and comparing the answers to see whether wording drives the result.

Overconfidence Bias
Confidence that exceeds accuracy. In AI use it is often driven by length, detail, and an unhedged tone.

Substitution Bias

Sycophancy Bias

System 1
