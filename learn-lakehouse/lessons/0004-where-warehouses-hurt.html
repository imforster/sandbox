Where warehouses hurt

Lesson 4 of 8. Time: 8 minutes. Tangible win: describe the two-tier architecture and its three costs.

The lakehouse exists because of three complaints about warehouses, as the authors state them in Section 3. These are the paper's claims, so note them as claims.

Three challenges

ChallengeWhat the paper says
Mostly structured dataWarehouses were built for structured, schema-on-write data. Machine learning wants JSON, images, and iterative training, so data scientists reach for the lake.
Lock-in and lock-outData sits in proprietary formats. Teams export it to a lake through ETL, which creates pipelines to maintain and extra copies.
High costStorage and compute costs grow over time, and pre-aggregated tables add more storage. Cost can limit analysis to a few datasets.

The two-tier architecture

The common workaround is two tiers: a data lake for raw and ML data, plus a warehouse for BI, with pipelines between them. The paper says this adds complexity and overhead, and that copies can cause data drift and model decay.

Read it critically

The paper says some cloud warehouses can query open-format external tables, but calls this complicated and slow. That is a claim about performance with no measurements in the paper.

Retrieval check

According to the paper, what causes unmanageable extra data copies?

Correct. Offloading warehouse data to a lake via ETL creates the copies and pipelines the paper criticizes.

Not quite. Dashboards and columnar formats are not blamed. The copies come from exporting warehouse data to a lake.

Practice

  1. Draw the two-tier architecture from memory and label every copy of the data.
  2. List the three challenges and tie each to a cost.
  3. Pick one challenge you could verify with real numbers, and note what you would measure.
